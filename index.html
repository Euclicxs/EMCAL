Formula Guide

Essential Electricity & Magnetism formulas with symbols, units, and descriptions.

Electric Charge
Q = ne
QElectric chargeCoulombs (C)
nNumber of electronsdimensionless
eElementary charge1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C

The total charge is the count of electrons multiplied by the fundamental charge of one electron.

Coulomb's Law
F = kq₁q₂ / r²
FElectrostatic forceNewtons (N)
kCoulomb's constant8.99×10⁹ N·m²/C²
q₁,q₂ChargesCoulombs (C)
rDistance between chargesMeters (m)

Describes the force between two point charges; inversely proportional to the square of distance.

Electric Field
E = F/q  |  E = kQ/r²
EElectric field strengthN/C or V/m
FForce on test chargeNewtons (N)
qTest chargeCoulombs (C)

The electric field is the force per unit charge experienced by a small positive test charge.

Ohm's Law
V = IR
VVoltageVolts (V)
ICurrentAmperes (A)
RResistanceOhms (Ω)

The voltage across a conductor equals the current through it times its resistance.

Electric Power
P = VI
PElectric powerWatts (W)
VVoltageVolts (V)
ICurrentAmperes (A)

Power is the rate of energy transfer; equals voltage multiplied by current.

Resistance
R = V / I
RResistanceOhms (Ω)
VVoltageVolts (V)
ICurrentAmperes (A)

Resistance is the opposition to current flow; derived from Ohm's Law.

Series Resistance
Rtotal = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + …
RₜTotal resistanceOhms (Ω)
R₁…Individual resistorsOhms (Ω)
ISame through allAmperes (A)

In series, resistances add directly. The same current flows through every component.

Parallel Resistance
1/Rtotal = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …
RₜTotal resistanceOhms (Ω)
R₁…Individual resistorsOhms (Ω)
VSame across allVolts (V)

In parallel, the total resistance is always less than the smallest individual resistor.

Magnetic Force
F = BIL sin θ  |  F = qvB sin θ
BMagnetic field strengthTesla (T)
ICurrentAmperes (A)
LLength of conductorMeters (m)
θAngle between B and currentDegrees (°)

Force on a current-carrying wire (or moving charge) in a magnetic field; zero when parallel.

📝 Worked Examples

Sample Problems

Step-by-step solutions for common Electricity & Magnetism problems.

Ohm's LawProblem 1
A resistor has a resistance of 47 Ω. If a current of 0.5 A flows through it, what is the voltage across it?
GivenR = 47 Ω,  I = 0.5 A
FormulaV = I × R
SolutionV = 0.5 × 47 = 23.5 V
✅ Voltage = 23.5 V
Electric PowerProblem 2
A light bulb operates at 120 V and draws 0.75 A of current. What is its power rating?
GivenV = 120 V,  I = 0.75 A
FormulaP = V × I
SolutionP = 120 × 0.75 = 90 W
✅ Power = 90 W
Coulomb's LawProblem 3
Two charges, q₁ = +3 μC and q₂ = −2 μC, are placed 0.05 m apart. What is the force between them?
Givenq₁ = 3×10⁻⁶ C,  q₂ = 2×10⁻⁶ C,  r = 0.05 m
FormulaF = kq₁q₂ / r²
SolutionF = (8.99×10⁹)(3×10⁻⁶)(2×10⁻⁶) / (0.05)² = 21.576 N
✅ Force = 21.58 N (attractive)
Electric FieldProblem 4
A point charge of Q = 5 μC creates an electric field at a point 0.3 m away. What is the field strength?
GivenQ = 5×10⁻⁶ C,  r = 0.3 m
FormulaE = kQ / r²
SolutionE = (8.99×10⁹)(5×10⁻⁶) / (0.3)² ≈ 499,444 N/C
✅ E ≈ 4.99 × 10⁵ N/C
Magnetic ForceProblem 5
A wire 0.4 m long carries a current of 5 A in a magnetic field of 0.3 T perpendicular to the wire. Find the force.
GivenB = 0.3 T,  I = 5 A,  L = 0.4 m,  θ = 90°
FormulaF = BIL sin θ
SolutionF = 0.3 × 5 × 0.4 × sin(90°) = 0.6 N
✅ Force = 0.6 N
Electric ChargeProblem 6
How many electrons must be transferred to give an object a charge of −1.6 × 10⁻¹⁷ C?
GivenQ = 1.6×10⁻¹⁷ C,  e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C
Formulan = Q / e
Solutionn = 1.6×10⁻¹⁷ / 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 100
✅ Electrons = 100
